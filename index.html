I am a Ph.D. candidate in condensed matter theory at the University of Hong Kong. My research explores topological phases, quantum transport, and unconventional superconductivity, combining analytical models with numerical calculations.

My work includes an exact analytical solution for magnetic topological insulator films, metallic quantum anomalous Hall and half-quantized mirror Hall phases, and ferroelectric control of topological superconductivity. In collaboration with computational and experimental groups, I also investigate half-quantized Hall responses and disorder-driven transport in magnetic topological insulators.

Recently, I coauthored a study of first-order topological FFLO transitions and superconducting diode sign reversal in altermagnetic nanowires (arXiv preprint). I am developing reliability-aware active learning to explore FFLO phase spaces, with a draft manuscript and research code on GitHub. I am also actively studying and researching quantum algorithms through analytical derivations, circuit implementations, and numerical experiments.

Topological FFLO Transitions and the Superconducting Diode Effect

Research collaboration · HKU · Preprint Jul 2026

  • Coauthored a study of spin-orbit-coupled nanowires proximitized to d-wave altermagnets, identifying a route to topological FFLO states at zero net magnetization.
  • Related competing pairing channels and a discontinuous switch of the free-energy minimum to a first-order topological FFLO transition, enhanced diode efficiency, and sharp diode sign reversal.
  • Connected the microscopic picture to a Ginzburg–Landau description and proposed the diode response as a transport signature of the transition.

arXiv:2607.15720 · Preprint PDF

🧪 Interests

Quantum algorithms — ongoing research: I am actively studying and researching quantum algorithms, with a particular interest in how their mathematical structure, circuit implementations, and computational complexity connect to problems in quantum physics. My current work includes analytical derivations and numerical experiments on quantum Fourier transforms, phase estimation, order finding, quantum search, and amplitude amplification. I document this work in my Quantum Algorithms notes and interactive companion with PennyLane experiments. I am interested in extending these foundations toward quantum simulation and algorithms for many-body systems.

Research: I am eager to contribute my expertise and collaborate on cutting-edge research in condensed matter physics. I look forward to the opportunity to further my research and make significant contributions to the field. First of all, I plan to continue to promote the exact analytical solution of the Schrödinger equation for complex systems to including interaction and disorder effects, which can serve as a benchmark for numerical simulations and provide deeper insights into the underlying physics. Secondly, I aim to explore the interplay between topology and symmetry in novel quantum materials, potentially leading to the discovery of new topological phases and transport phenomena. Meanwhile, I am interested in investigation of novel quantum orders and their potential applications in quantum computing and spintronics. Furthermore, I intend to apply machine learning techniques to analyze large datasets from experiments and simulations, uncovering hidden patterns and accelerating the discovery of new materials and phenomena. Finally, I intend to strengthen my collaboration with experimental groups to validate theoretical predictions and contribute to the advancement of condensed matter physics. I am also open to interdisciplinary research opportunities that allow me to apply my skills and knowledge to other fields, such as quantum information science, materials science, and computational physics.

Daily: Psychology, philosophy, music, movies, games, hiking, traveling, etc. I have been quite interested in works of Arthur Schopenhauer, Carl Jung, Albert Camus, Owen Yaron and so on. Nevertheless, I prefer more to actually experience the world and interact with people rather than just reading about them, and I am always open to new experiences and enjoy exploring different cultures and perspectives. I think that's why I have been quite interested in traveling and hiking, which allow me to connect with nature and different cultures, and also provide a refreshing break from academic work. I believe that a well-rounded life outside of academia can enrich my research and personal growth, and I am eager to connect with others who share similar interests.
